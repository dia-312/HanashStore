# Sales Rep App — SPEC

Last updated: 29/10/2026

## 1. Overview

An installable web app (PWA) for one wholesale sales rep who sells hookahs, hoses, charcoal and accessories to shops. He runs it on his own iPad while visiting customers: he shows the catalog, the customer fills a cart, the rep confirms an invoice (with whatever the customer pays now) and sends it to the customer as a PDF on WhatsApp. The app also tracks stock, customer accounts (invoices, payments, returns, balance) and sales reports.

The two things that matter most to the client: knowing exactly what is in stock, and making it easy to show products to customers.

Fixed constraints (do not change without asking):

- **Speed is a top goal.** The app must feel instant on the iPad. Section 17 sets the budgets and the rules; they win over convenience.
- **One user** (the rep, who is also the owner and admin). No roles or permissions system. One device only: his own iPad.
- **Password once, then a PIN.** No Face ID.
- **iPad Safari, installed to the Home Screen** as a PWA. No native app, no App Store.
- **Must work offline.** Showing the catalog, building a cart, confirming an invoice, recording a payment and recording a return all work with no internet and sync later.
- **Wholesale only.** One selling price per variant. No retail prices, no customer-specific price lists.
- **Currency: ILS (₪) only.** No VAT: invoices are plain sales invoices, not tax invoices.
- **One stock location** (the warehouse). No van stock, no transfers.
- **No overselling.** The app must not let the rep sell more than is in stock.
- **No cost prices** and no profit reports.
- **No product codes from the client.** SKUs are generated by the app.
- **Customers are added one by one** by the rep as he starts dealing with them. No bulk customer import.
- **Products come from the client's Excel file** for the initial load.
- **Arabic UI, right-to-left, Latin digits (0–9) everywhere.** All code, database names, API paths and comments in English.
- **Catalog size:** 200–300 products today; design for up to 2,000 variants.
- **Discount:** one fixed amount (not a percentage) on the whole invoice, entered at checkout.
- **Returns:** required in the MVP.
- **Cheques:** out of scope for now (see Later).

## 4. MVP features

Thirteen features. Each is a user story for the rep, followed by acceptance criteria that tests must cover.

**F1. Login and app lock.** As the rep, I want the app protected so a lost iPad does not expose customer debts.

- Login with password, once per device (needs internet). The session lasts 30 days and renews itself on every sync.
- Daily use is the 4–6 digit PIN: on app open, after 5 minutes idle, or when reopened after 5 minutes in the background, the app shows a lock screen that asks for the PIN. Works offline. No Face ID (client decision).
- Only one device is active at a time. Logging in on a new device (e.g. a replacement iPad) asks for confirmation, then revokes the old one; a revoked device wipes its local data on its next contact with the server. A "log out everywhere" action works from any browser in case the iPad is lost. Unsynced changes on a lost iPad are lost, which is why section 8 warns about pending changes.
- Logout wipes IndexedDB and the service worker caches on that device. Logout is blocked while there are unsynced commands. An expired session never wipes anything: the rep logs in again and the outbox is kept.

**F2. Products, variants and categories.** As the rep, I want to manage my catalog with colors and sizes.

- Create, edit and deactivate categories, products and variants. Nothing with history is ever hard-deleted.
- A product has: name, category, description, images (ordered). A variant has: SKU, color, size, selling price, minimum stock level, optional own image.
- Color + size is unique within a product. SKU is unique overall and always auto-generated (the client has no product codes). It is never required in forms or import.
- Images are uploaded from the iPad camera or photos, resized on the server, and shown as thumbnails in lists.
- Excel (.xlsx) and CSV import for the initial catalog (product, category, color, size, price, opening quantity), with a preview showing errors per row before saving. The client will provide his own Excel file: map its real columns once received; don't make him reformat it.
- Catalog editing (including images) requires internet; the UI disables it offline with a clear message.

**F3. Stock.** As the rep, I want to know exactly what is available and why each number is what it is.

- Stock per variant = sum of its stock movements. Never edited directly.
- Screens: stock in (receive goods: pick many variants fast, enter quantity each, optional note/supplier), adjustment after a count (enter the counted quantity; the app records the difference with a required reason), mark damaged.
- Each variant shows its movement history: date, type, quantity, reference (invoice/return number), note, running total.
- Low stock = quantity ≤ minimum stock level. Out of stock = quantity ≤ 0.

**F4. Customers.** As the rep, I want a profile for every shop with its full history.

- Fields: name, shop name, phone, area, address, notes, optional opening balance (for a customer who already owes money when first added). The rep adds customers one by one; there is no bulk customer import.
- Profile shows: balance (large), call and WhatsApp buttons, actions ("عرض للزبون" = start a sale in presentation mode, record payment, new return, statement), and tabs: invoices, payments, returns, statement, frequently bought products.
- Customer list: search by name/shop/phone/area (Arabic-normalized), sort by name, balance, or last invoice date.
- Customer create/edit works offline.

**F5. Catalog browsing and search (rep mode).** As the rep, I want to find any item in seconds.

- Grid and list views; each item shows image, name, price, stock quantity, low/out badge.
- Search and filters as specified in section 9. Works fully offline.

**F6. Presentation mode.** As the rep, I want to open the catalog for the customer at every visit and let him fill his own cart. This is the main sales flow. Specified in section 10.

**F7. Cart and invoice.** As the rep, I want to build an order for a customer and turn it into an invoice.

- A cart belongs to one customer. Opening presentation mode from a customer opens or resumes that customer's cart.
- Carts are saved locally as drafts and survive app restarts; one open cart per customer.
- Add items from presentation mode (main flow), the rep catalog, search, or "repeat last invoice". Change quantity with a stepper or by typing; remove lines.
- Cart shows per line: product, variant, quantity, unit price, line total; then subtotal. Lines use the current local price; the price is frozen on the invoice at confirm.
- Overselling is blocked: out-of-stock variants can't be added, the quantity can't go above available stock, and confirm is disabled while any line exceeds stock (see section 7).
- Checkout (section 10): optional discount, then the amount the customer pays now (0 up to any amount; partial payment is normal) and its method. The summary shows total, paid now, remaining on this invoice, customer's current balance and balance after. Confirming creates the invoice and, if the amount is above zero, a payment linked to it, in one step; deducts stock; updates the customer's balance; and empties the cart. Works offline.
- After confirming, the invoice screen opens with "Send on WhatsApp".

**F8. Invoice actions.** As the rep, I want to manage invoices after they are issued.

- Invoice list: filter by customer, date range, delivered/not delivered, status.
- Invoice view: all details (including paid and remaining), send PDF, mark as delivered (records time only, no stock effect), cancel with a required reason (only if it has no returns), create a return.
- Invoices are never edited. To fix one: cancel and issue a new one, or create a return.

**F9. Send invoice and statement on WhatsApp.** Specified in section 11.

**F10. Payments.** As the rep, I want to record money received.

- Fields: customer, amount (ILS), method (cash, bank transfer, cheque), date, note. For cheques in the MVP, the rep writes cheque details (number, bank, date) in the note.
- A payment reduces the customer's balance. Overpayment is allowed and shows as credit (negative balance).
- Payments are voided with a reason, never deleted. Works offline.
- Payments taken at checkout are linked to their invoice; payments on a visit with no new order (paying off old debt) are recorded from the customer profile.

**F11. Returns.** As the rep, I want to take items back from a customer.

- A return starts from an invoice of that customer. Invoices older than 12 months need internet, since they aren't kept on the device. The rep picks lines, quantity per line, and for each: back to stock or damaged.
- The credited amount per line is calculated by the rules in section 7 and shown before confirming.
- Confirming reduces the customer's balance and, for "back to stock", adds stock. Works offline.

**F12. Dashboard and reports.** As the rep, I want to see how sales and stock are going.

- Dashboard: today's sales total and invoice count, today's collections by method (for counting cash at the end of the day), this month's sales vs last month, total owed by all customers, low-stock count, out-of-stock count, unsynced changes count.
- Reports with date range: sales by day and by month (chart + totals), sales by product/variant/category, top and slow sellers, collections by method, customer balances (sorted, with days since last payment), low and out of stock list, damaged items.
- Sales means invoice totals minus returns in the period, excluding cancelled invoices.
- Reports need internet (computed on the server); the dashboard shows the last synced values offline.

**F13. Data export and backup.** As the rep, I want my data safe.

- Settings → export all data as a ZIP of CSV files.
- Daily automatic database backup on the server (see section 13).

- Multiple reps, roles and permissions.
- Retail prices or customer-specific price lists.
- Percentage or per-line discounts.
- Returns not linked to an invoice.
- Cost prices and profit reports.

## 6. Data model

The PostgreSQL schema below is the source of truth; the Dexie schema on the device mirrors the tables the device needs. Conventions:

- Primary keys are UUIDs **generated on the client** (needed for offline creation). Use UUIDv7 for time ordering.
- Money is `bigint` in **agorot** (1 ILS = 100 agorot). Never floats, anywhere: DB, API, or client math.
- Quantities are integers.
- Timestamps are `timestamptz` stored in UTC, displayed in `Asia/Hebron`.
- Every table that syncs to devices has `updated_at` and a `change_seq bigint` set on every insert/update (used by sync pull, see section 8). Rows are never hard-deleted from synced tables; removals use `is_active` or `deleted_at` so the device learns about them.
- Financial records (invoices, lines, returns, payments, stock movements) are append-only. They are never updated except for the status fields listed below, and never deleted.

```sql

  id uuid pk, name text not null, password_hash text not null,
  pin_hash text, created_at timestamptz not null
)
devices (
  id uuid pk, name text not null,                       -- only one active device at a time
  last_seen_at timestamptz, revoked_at timestamptz, created_at timestamptz
)
settings (
  key text pk, value jsonb not null, updated_at timestamptz
)  -- business_name, phone, logo_url, invoice_footer, hide_out_of_stock_in_presentation
sync_clock (
  id int pk check (id = 1), last_seq bigint not null     -- locked FOR UPDATE by every write transaction
)

categories (
  id uuid pk, name text not null, sort_order int not null default 0,
  is_active bool not null default true, updated_at, change_seq
)
products (

product_images (
```

Indexes: `stock_movements (variant_id, created_at)`, `invoices (customer_id, issued_at desc)`, `payments (customer_id, paid_at desc)`, `payments (invoice_id)`, `returns (customer_id)`, `invoice_lines (invoice_id)`, `invoice_lines (variant_id)`, `return_lines (invoice_line_id)`, `change_seq` on every synced table, GIN pg_trgm on `products.search_text` and `customers.search_text`.

`product_variants.stock_qty` is a cache. It is updated in the same database transaction as every stock movement insert. A management command `recompute_stock` rebuilds it from `stock_movements` and reports any mismatch; a test asserts the cache always equals the sum.

## 7. Business rules

These rules live in one backend service module and one shared frontend module, each with unit tests. The frontend copy exists only so offline previews match; the server always recalculates and its result wins.

**Money**

- All amounts are integer agorot. Display as `₪ 1,250.00` (Latin digits, comma thousands).
- Inputs accept up to 2 decimal places and are converted to agorot immediately.

**Stock**

- Variant stock = sum of `stock_movements.qty`. Movement signs: opening +, purchase +, sale −, sale_cancel +, return (restock) +, damage −, adjustment ±.
- An adjustment stores the difference between counted and current quantity, with a required note.
- Overselling is blocked (client decision). The device checks against its local stock, which already includes its own pending sales, when adding to the cart and again on confirm. Carts do not reserve stock. Damage and adjustment can't take stock below zero either. The server re-checks on `invoice.create`. With one device, stock can't go negative. If the server's re-check ever fails anyway (a bug or a direct data fix), it still applies the invoice (the sale really happened), returns a `STOCK_NEGATIVE` warning, and the variant shows in red on the dashboard.

**Invoices**

- Created only by confirming a cart at checkout. On confirm, in one transaction: insert invoice and lines with name and price snapshots, insert one `sale` movement per line, update stock cache, insert the payment if an amount was paid now (linked by `invoice_id`), write audit log.
- `line_total = qty × unit_price`; `subtotal = Σ line_total`; `total = subtotal − discount`.
- Discount: fixed agorot, `0 ≤ discount ≤ subtotal`.
- Discount allocation: split the discount across lines in proportion to `line_total`, rounded down to the agora, then give the leftover agorot one by one to lines with the largest remainders. The sum of `discount_share` must equal `discount` exactly.
- Invoices are immutable. Allowed changes: set `delivered_at`; cancel.
- Cancel: only if the invoice has no returns. Requires a reason. Inserts `sale_cancel` movements reversing each line. A cancelled invoice no longer counts in balances or sales reports, but stays visible marked as cancelled.

**Returns**

- A return line references an invoice line. `qty ≤ sold qty − qty already returned` on that line.
- Credit per return line: `amount = floor((line_total − discount_share) × qty_returned / qty_sold)`. When a return brings the line's total returned qty to the full sold qty, `amount` = remaining unreturned value of the line instead, so the line's returns add up exactly to `line_total − discount_share`.
- `restock` inserts a `return` movement (+qty). `damaged` inserts no movement; the item is recorded in the return line and appears in the damaged report.
- The customer is credited the amount in both cases.

**Payments**

- `amount > 0`. Voiding sets `status = voided` with a reason; voided payments don't count in balances.
- **Refunds** (added after the M7 iPad check, client request): money handed back to a customer, usually after a return he had already paid for. Stored in `payments` with `kind = 'refund'` (default `'payment'`), not linked to an invoice, number `REF-000003`, command `refund.create`. Voided like a payment (`payment.void`), with a reason. In the balance an active refund counts the other way: it raises what he owes (or lowers his credit). In the statement it is a debit row ("استرداد"); a voided one is shown only with the toggle. A refund may be larger than his credit (he then owes); the app only warns.

**Paid at checkout**

- Paid now can be any amount ≥ 0, including more than the invoice total (the customer also paying old debt). If it leaves the customer in credit, show a warning before confirming.
- Paid on an invoice = sum of active payments linked to it. Remaining on an invoice = max(total − paid, 0).
- Later payments are not allocated to specific invoices. The customer balance and statement are the source of truth for what he owes.
- Cancelling an invoice that has a linked payment asks the rep: void the payment too (money given back) or keep it as credit on the customer's balance.

**Customer balance**

```text
balance = opening_balance
        + Σ total of confirmed invoices
        − Σ total of returns
        − Σ amount of active payments
        + Σ amount of active refunds
```

Positive = customer owes. Negative = customer has credit. Calculated by query, never stored. The device caches the last server value and adjusts it locally for pending commands.

**Statement (كشف حساب)**

- Chronological rows: opening balance, invoices (debit), returns (credit), payments (credit), refunds (debit), with running balance. Cancelled invoices and voided payments are hidden by default, shown with a toggle.

**Document numbers**

- Format `{TYPE}-{6-digit sequence}`: `INV-000042`, `RET-000007`, `REC-000010`.
- The device keeps a counter per type in IndexedDB, so numbers are generated offline. When a device is registered (first login or a replacement iPad), it starts its counters from the server's highest numbers. The server rejects a duplicate number (should never happen) and the device then increments and retries.

**Time**

- `issued_at` / `paid_at` = the device clock at the moment of the action, not the sync time. Displayed as `dd/MM/yyyy HH:mm` in Asia/Hebron.

**Deletion**

- Nothing financial is ever deleted. Products, variants, categories and customers with history can only be deactivated. Inactive items are hidden from catalog and pickers but stay in history and reports.

## 8. Offline and sync

The server is the source of truth; the device holds a full working copy and an outbox of commands. Every write the rep makes in the field goes through the same command path, online or offline, so there is one code path to test.

**What lives on the device (Dexie)**

- All active categories, products, variants (with `stock_qty`), image metadata, customers, synonym groups, settings.
- Invoices, lines, returns, payments for the last 12 months; older ones are fetched from the server on demand.
- Customer balances as last reported by the server.
- Carts (local only, never synced).
- Outbox of pending commands; document number counters.

**Pull (server → device)**

- `GET /api/v1/sync/pull?cursor=<change_seq>` returns all rows with `change_seq > cursor` across synced tables, up to 1,000 rows per page, plus the new cursor and `has_more`. First sync uses cursor 0.
- `change_seq` must follow commit order, otherwise a slow transaction could commit a lower number after the device already moved past it and that row would never sync. So every write transaction takes its numbers from the single-row `sync_clock` table locked with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, which serializes writers (cheap with one user).
- The device applies rows in a single Dexie transaction per page, then rebuilds the search index if catalog rows changed.

**Push (device → server)**

1. Each write creates a command `{id, type, schema_version, payload, created_at}` with a client UUID, applies it to local data right away (optimistic), and stores it in the outbox.
2. `POST /api/v1/sync/push` sends pending commands in creation order, up to 50 per request.
3. The server processes each in its own transaction. If `processed_commands` already has the id, it returns the stored result (idempotent). Otherwise it validates, applies, stores the result.
4. Response per command: `applied` (with server-calculated values) or `rejected` (with error code and message). Applied commands leave the outbox. Rejected commands stay in a "Sync issues" screen where the rep sees what failed and why, and can discard or fix and retry.
5. After every push, run a pull.

Command types in the MVP: `customer.upsert`, `invoice.create`, `invoice.mark_delivered`, `invoice.cancel`, `return.create`, `payment.create`, `payment.void`, `refund.create`, `stock.purchase`, `stock.adjust`, `stock.damage`.

`invoice.create` carries an optional payment; `invoice.cancel` carries `void_payment` (true or false). Catalog management (products, variants, categories, images, settings, synonyms) is online-only through normal REST endpoints, because image uploads need the network anyway.

**When to sync**

- On app start, when the browser fires `online`, every 60 seconds while online and the app is open, and on a manual "Sync now" button.
- iOS Safari has no Background Sync: syncing only happens while the app is open. The UI must make pending changes visible.

**Sync status (always visible in the header)**

- Synced ✓ / N pending / Offline / Error. Tapping it opens the sync screen: pending commands, last successful sync time, issues.
- If any command has been pending for more than 24 hours, show a persistent warning banner.

**Conflicts**

- Financial records are append-only and created with unique client IDs, so they never conflict.
- Customer and product field edits: last write wins by `updated_at`. Acceptable for one user.
- There is only one device, so there are no stock races between devices.

**Images and storage on iOS**

- Service worker precaches the app shell. All product images (thumb and full) are downloaded and cached automatically after each pull, because presentation mode is the main sales flow and must show every image offline.
- Settings shows image download progress and a "Re-download all images" button.
- Call `navigator.storage.persist()` after login and show the result in Settings.
- The app must be used from the Home Screen icon. If opened in a Safari tab, show a banner explaining how to install it, because Safari can clear data of non-installed sites.

**App updates**

- A new deployment shows "تحديث متوفر"; the update applies on the next reload, never in the middle of a cart or checkout.
- Dexie schema changes use versioned migrations that keep the outbox, carts and counters.
- Every command carries `schema_version`. The server accepts the current and the previous version, so a device with old pending commands can still sync after an update.

## 9. Arabic search

Search runs on the device with MiniSearch over normalized text, expanded by an editable synonym list, with typo tolerance. The client marked correct Arabic search as essential, so it ships with a fixed test suite.

**Normalization** — one function, `normalizeArabic(text)`, implemented identically in TypeScript and Python, applied to both indexed text and queries:

1. Lowercase Latin letters.
2. Remove tashkeel (U+064B–U+065F, U+0670) and tatweel (U+0640).
3. أ إ آ ٱ → ا; ة → ه; ى → ي; ؤ → و; ئ → ي.
4. Arabic-Indic digits ٠–٩ and Persian digits ۰–۹ → 0–9.
5. Replace punctuation with spaces; collapse whitespace; trim.

Both implementations run against the same fixture file `shared/normalize_cases.json` (input → expected output) in their test suites, so they can't drift.

**Indexed text per variant** — normalized: product name, category name, color, size, SKU, description. Results are grouped and shown per product, with matching variants highlighted.

**Synonyms** — `synonym_groups` editable in Settings. If a query token is in a group, search all terms of that group (OR). Seed groups:

- ارجيله، اركيله، شيشه، نرجيله
- بربيش، لي، خرطوم
- فحم، فحمات
- راس، حجر
- ماسك، مبسم

**Matching** — tokens combined with AND; prefix match on every token (typing "ارج" finds أرجيلة); fuzzy match with 1 edit for tokens of 4+ characters; SKU exact match ranks first. Target: results in under 50 ms for 2,000 variants on an iPad.

**Filters** — combinable chips above results: category, color, size, availability (in stock / low / out), price range. **Sort:** relevance (default when searching), name, best-selling (last 90 days), newest, price. Active filters persist until the rep clears them or leaves the catalog.

**Required test cases** (must all pass):

| Query | Must find |
| --- | --- |
| ارجيله | أرجيلة ... |
| اركيلة | أرجيلة ... (synonym) |
| شيشة | أرجيلة ... (synonym) |
| بربيش اسود | black hose variants only |
| ارج | all hookahs (prefix) |
| ارجيلا | أرجيلة ... (typo) |
| فحم ٢٥ | charcoal variants with size 25 (Arabic digit) |
| SKU in any case | that variant first |

## 10. Presentation mode

A full-screen, customer-safe catalog the rep hands over at every visit. It shows products and prices only, the customer fills his own cart, and only the rep (with the PIN) can leave it or check out.

**Entry — the main sales flow.** At each visit the rep opens the customer's profile and taps "عرض للزبون". Presentation mode opens with that customer active and his open cart, if any. It can also be opened from the catalog with no customer; the customer is then picked at checkout.

**Layout**

- Home: large category tiles with an image each.
- Category: grid of 3 columns (portrait) or 4 (landscape), big image, product name, price per unit, availability badge.
- Product detail: swipeable image gallery, color swatches and size buttons; choosing a variant switches to its image if it has one.
- Search bar and category/color filters available (same engine as section 9).

**Shown:** product name, images, description, selling price, available colors and sizes, availability as a badge only: متوفر (in stock), كمية محدودة (stock ≤ min level), غير متوفر (out). Out-of-stock items are hidden by default (setting); when shown, they can't be added.

**Never shown:** exact stock quantity, any customer's name other than the active one, any balance, invoices, payments, menus, navigation, settings, sync status details.

**Adding to cart** — an "أضف" button with a quantity stepper on each available variant; the stepper can't go above available stock. A floating cart badge shows the item count. Its sheet lists items, quantities and the total before discount, and lets the customer change quantities or remove items. A "خلصت" button hands the iPad back to the rep.

1. Review the lines (the rep can still edit them).
2. Pick the customer, if none is active.
3. Discount: fixed amount, optional.
4. Amount paid now: required field, 0 allowed, any amount above 0 allowed. Method: cash (default), transfer or cheque, plus an optional note (required for cheque: number, bank, date). If the amount leaves the customer in credit, show a warning before confirming.

## 11. Invoice and statement on WhatsApp

Online, the server renders an A4 PDF with WeasyPrint and the iPad share sheet sends it to WhatsApp; offline, the device renders the same layout as a PNG image and shares that instead. This is the highest technical risk and is proven first in the spike (section 15).

**Invoice contents (RTL, Arabic, A4 portrait)**

- Header: business name, logo, phone (from settings).
- Invoice number, date and time (`dd/MM/yyyy HH:mm`), "cancelled" stamp if cancelled.
- Customer: name, shop name, phone.
- Lines table: #, product, variant (color/size), quantity, unit price, line total.
- Subtotal, discount (only if > 0), total, paid now, remaining on this invoice.
- No overall customer balance on the invoice (client decision). No VAT line.
- Footer note (from settings).

**Online flow**

1. `GET /api/v1/invoices/{id}/pdf` returns `application/pdf`, rendered from a Jinja2 template with the Arabic font embedded.
2. The client builds a `File` named `INV-000042.pdf` and calls `navigator.share({ files: [file] })`. The rep picks WhatsApp and the customer's chat.
3. If `navigator.canShare({files})` is false, fall back to opening the PDF in a new tab.

**Offline flow**

1. A React component renders the same invoice layout off-screen.
2. html-to-image converts it to PNG at 2× scale.
3. Shared with `navigator.share` as `INV-000042.png`. The invoice screen shows that the PDF can be sent later when online.

The Jinja2 template and the React component must look the same. Keep both small and keep their styles in one shared CSS file used by both.

**Statement** — the same two flows for a customer statement (`GET /api/v1/customers/{id}/statement.pdf?from=&to=`), defaulting to the last 3 months. Used to chase debts on WhatsApp.

**Spike must confirm on the real iPad, as a Home Screen app:** file sharing to WhatsApp works for both PDF and PNG; Arabic letters join correctly in the PDF; numbers and Latin text keep the right order inside RTL text.

## 12. API

## 13. Security and non-functional requirements

The app holds every customer's debt on a device that travels, so security, backups and correctness of money rules come before features.

**Security**

- HTTPS only (Caddy, automatic certificates). HSTS on.
- Session cookie: `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Lax`, 30-day sliding expiry. Every state-changing request must also send header `X-Requested-With: app`; the server rejects requests without it (CSRF protection).
- Passwords hashed with argon2id. Login rate limit: 5 attempts per minute per IP, then 15-minute lockout.
- PIN: 4–6 digits, hashed with a salt. A copy of the hash is kept on the device so unlock works offline. After 5 wrong PIN attempts: online, require the full password; offline, lock with growing delays (1, 5, then 15 minutes). A short PIN hash on the device can be brute-forced if the iPad's storage is extracted, one more reason the iPad passcode is required.
- Revoked device: server answers 401 `DEVICE_REVOKED`; the client wipes IndexedDB, caches and cookies.
- Local data in IndexedDB is not encrypted by the app. Document clearly that the iPad itself must have a passcode.
- Secrets only in environment variables; commit `.env.example`, never `.env`.
- Validate every input at the boundary: zod on the client, Pydantic on the server. String max lengths on all text fields. Quantities and amounts are integers within sane bounds (qty ≤ 100,000; amount ≤ ₪10,000,000).
- Image uploads: accept JPEG, PNG, HEIC, WebP up to 10 MB; re-encode with Pillow (strips EXIF and location data).
- Audit log entry for every command applied, every price change, every cancel and void.

**Backups**

- Daily `pg_dump` at 03:00 Asia/Hebron to R2, kept 30 days. Images are already in R2.
- A documented, tested restore procedure in `docs/RESTORE.md`.

**Performance:** see section 17. It is a top goal, not a nice-to-have.

**Testing (required before a milestone is "done")**

- Backend unit tests: discount allocation, return amounts, paid/remaining, balance, stock sums, document numbers, normalization fixtures.
- Backend integration tests: every command type, idempotency (same command twice = one effect), rejection cases, sync pull ordering.
- Frontend unit tests: normalization fixtures, search cases from section 9, cart and checkout math.
- Playwright end-to-end: login → add customer → fill cart in presentation mode → checkout with a partial payment offline → go online → sync → invoice and payment exist on server with correct totals, balance and stock.
- Performance: a seed script loads the large test dataset from section 17; the budgets there are checked on the real iPad at the end of every milestone.
- CI runs lint, typecheck, all tests and the bundle-size check on every push.

**Locale**

- `lang="ar" dir="rtl"`. Latin digits (0–9) everywhere. Dates `dd/MM/yyyy`, Gregorian. Timezone Asia/Hebron.

## 17. Performance (top priority)

The app must feel instant on the client's iPad. The core rule: **the screen never waits for the network.** Every screen reads from IndexedDB and renders right away; the network only syncs in the background.

**Budgets (measured on the client's iPad, from the Home Screen app, with the large test dataset)**

| What | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Cold start (app closed) to PIN screen | < 1.5 s |
| PIN entered to usable catalog | < 0.5 s |
| Opening any screen (customer, invoice, product) | < 300 ms |
| Search results while typing | < 50 ms |
| Scrolling the catalog grid (2,000 variants) | Smooth, no visible stutter |
| Adding to cart, changing quantity | Instant (< 50 ms) |
| Confirming an invoice offline | < 300 ms |
| Invoice PDF ready to share (online) | < 2 s |
| Push of 50 commands (online) | < 3 s |
| API response, 95th percentile | < 200 ms |
| Initial JavaScript (app shell, gzipped) | < 250 KB |

**Large test dataset** (seed script, used for every milestone check): 2,000 variants across 400 products with images, 500 customers, 20,000 invoices with lines, 15,000 payments, 1,000 returns.

**Rules for the frontend**

- Render from Dexie first, sync after first paint. Startup never waits for a pull, a push or an image download.
- Long lists (catalog, customers, invoices, statement, movement history) are virtualized with @tanstack/react-virtual. Never render a full list.
- Dexie queries use indexes and load only what the screen shows (paged or limited); no loading whole tables into components.
- Search index: MiniSearch index is built once, saved to IndexedDB with `toJSON`, and loaded with `loadJSON` at startup. It is rebuilt in a Web Worker only when catalog rows change.
- Search input uses `useDeferredValue` so typing never lags behind results.
- Heavy work runs off the main thread or in small idle chunks: applying large pulls, rebuilding the search index, prefetching images.
- Image prefetch runs in the background with at most 4 parallel downloads and pauses while the rep is in checkout.
- Images: thumbnails are 400px WebP (aim for ~25 KB), `loading="lazy"`, `decoding="async"`, fixed aspect-ratio boxes so nothing jumps. Full 1200px images only in product detail and the gallery.
- Code splitting: reports, charts, html-to-image, Excel import, settings and the stock screens load on demand with dynamic `import()`. The sales path (PIN, catalog, presentation mode, cart, checkout, invoice) is in the main bundle and precached.
- Keep re-renders small: Zustand selectors, `React.memo` on grid and list items, stable keys.
- No heavy UI or date libraries. Use `Intl.NumberFormat` and `Intl.DateTimeFormat` for money and dates.
- Fonts: self-hosted woff2 subset, preloaded, `font-display: swap`.

**Rules for the backend and hosting**

- Indexes from section 6 are required; every list endpoint is paginated.
- Reports are computed with SQL aggregates, never by loading rows into Python.
- PDF rendering: load the Jinja2 template and the font once at startup, not per request.
- Caddy: brotli/gzip on, and `Cache-Control: immutable` for hashed static files and images.
- VPS in a region close to Palestine (e.g. Frankfurt) to keep round trips short.

**How it is checked**

- CI fails if the initial JavaScript bundle goes over budget.
- At the end of every milestone, the budgets table is checked on the real iPad with the large test dataset, and the results are written in the milestone notes in `CLAUDE.md`.
